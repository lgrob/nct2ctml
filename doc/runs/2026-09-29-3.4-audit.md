## 2. Spot check of 60 published trials

Sample: `python -m utils.review_helper audit --n 60` (seed 20260925) -
60 of 916 trials that are published as `mapped`, neither reviewed nor queued
(46 ClinicalTrials.gov, 14 CTIS). Sheets in `review_sheets/audit/index.html`, verdict sheet
`ctml/audit_2026-09-29.tsv`.

Method. For each trial the auditor model was given the registry inclusion and exclusion text,
the conditions and titles, the registry age fields, and everything the pipeline published
(every arm's match tree, the age bounds, `genes_not_required`), plus the index's own contract:
subtrees expand downstream, `_SOLID_`/`_LIQUID_` are deliberate, `!` means exclusion,
`genes_not_required` is deliberate, arms may differ. It returned a per-criterion verdict with a
verbatim quote for every disagreement. Quotes were then checked against the supplied text in
code (11 of 94 were not verbatim and those flags were set aside).

First pass: 94 issues on 53 of 60 trials;
28 trials were marked not supported. After setting aside the non-verbatim
quotes, the systematic age convention (below) and the registry data errors (below),
45 trials carried an open flag, 30 of them with a screening effect.

**Then I adjudicated 20 of those 60 against the text myself**: all 10 with a high-confidence
screening-effect flag, and 10 of the remaining 20 drawn at random (seed 20260929).

| | adjudicated | real | borderline | false positive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| high-confidence flags | 10 | 9 | 0 | 1 |
| medium/low flags | 10 of 20 | 5 | 2 | 3 |
| total | 20 | 14 | 2 | 4 |

First-pass precision on what I checked: **14 of 20 (70%, 95% CI 46-88%)**.

### Estimated error rate for unreviewed rows

Taking the high-confidence stratum as counted (9 of 10 real) and extrapolating the
medium/low stratum from its sample (5 of 10 real, so 10 of 20), and assuming no error in the
30 trials with no screening-effect flag:

> **about 19 of 60 published trials carry at least one criterion a curator would change:
> 32% (95% CI 21-42%).**
> Of those, the ones that lose eligible patients rather than over-matching:
> about 11 of 60, **18%**.

This is far above the target the 60-trial sample size was chosen for (0 errors in 60 would have
bounded the rate at about 5%). Two caveats in opposite directions: the 30 trials the first pass
passed were not verified, so false negatives are not measured; and "would change" includes
scope judgements a curator may well leave alone.

## 3. Findings, by cause

Confirmed by reading the trial text. Each names one trial from the sample; the cause is general.

1. **The conditions field is not a diagnosis, so the basket wildcards fire.** NCT04217512:
   conditions are `['Oncology']`, the inclusion text is "Patients with head and neck cancer",
   and the published tree is `_SOLID_ + _LIQUID_ + Head and Neck Carcinoma, Other` - all 879
   OncoTree names, i.e. the trial matches every patient. This is the `no_diagnosis` failure mode
   passing the check that is meant to catch it.
2. **A diagnosis read out of a non-diagnostic sentence.** NCT07262489 is a basket master
   protocol for any "Localized solid malignancy"; it is published as Hepatocellular Carcinoma
   only, because HCC appears once inside a bilirubin threshold exception. Roadmap 2.7
   (quote-grounded diagnoses) is aimed exactly at this.
3. **Abbreviation collision in the conditions seed.** NCT07188441 lists the bare string `GCT`;
   the trial is a central malignant germ cell tumour study and it is published as
   `Germ Cell Tumor, Brain` **and** `Granular Cell Tumor`.
4. **Wrong branch.** NCT06411821, a histiocytic-neoplasm trial (conditions
   `['Histiocytic Neoplasms']`), is published as three unrelated lymphoma codes. The
   wrong-branch problem in `doc/open_issues.md`, still costing recall.
5. **Unqualified ALL lineage, in both directions.** NCT07070219 (T-ALL/LBL only) and
   NCT07070323 (CD5+ T-cell malignancies) both carry B-lymphoblastic codes, while NCT02508038
   ("high-risk refractory or relapsed ALL", no lineage) carries only B-lymphoblastic and loses
   T-ALL patients. The 1.x rule that adds T-ALL beside B-ALL neither fired where it should nor
   held back where the text names a lineage.
6. **`disease_status` applied where the text does not support it.** NCT02446431 requires
   `Recurrent` although the trial enrols patients *in remission* and excludes relapsed or
   refractory disease. NCT03314974 requires Recurrent/Refractory/Advanced on every diagnosis
   although this transplant trial enrols CR1 patients.
7. **Exclusions not written into the tree.** NCT03630991 excludes APL in the text, but the
   published AML parent covers APL and no `!` exclusion was written. NCT04072042 publishes
   Liposarcoma although the text excludes adipocytic tumours.
8. **Registry age field against the prose.** NCT04616560 publishes `age_min` 12 y from the
   registry field; the text admits Wilms and DSRCT patients from 2 y in the phase 2 part.
9. **No OncoTree code for the stated population.** 2024-520436-15-00 admits "GD2+ tumors other
   than Neuroblastoma" in phase II; only Neuroblastoma is published. There is no code for that
   population, so this needs a curation rule (a wildcard, or the queue), not a mapping fix.

### Two corpus-wide defects found while auditing

**A. The published upper age bound is one unit wider than the trial states, for
496 of 765 trials that have one.** The ClinicalTrials.gov path adds one completed unit to an
inclusive maximum ("40 years or younger" admits until the 41st birthday) and keeps the `<=`
operator: `src/clinical_trials_gov.py` builds `<=41`. That is harmless in MatchMiner, whose
engine treats `<` and `<=` identically - the comment there says so - but the flat index reads
the operator literally and publishes `age_max = 41, age_max_inclusive = 1`. A consumer joining
on integer years admits a whole extra year of patients. `utils/age_bounds.prose_bounds` already
does it correctly (`<N+1`), which is why the 94 prose-corrected CTGOV trials and all
175 CTIS trials are exclusive. Fix: emit `<` on the registry path, or have the index treat a
`<=` from that path as exclusive. One line either way, no model calls, and the benchmark cannot
see it (age is scored as a label).

**B. Three trials inherit a units error from the registry itself.** Each states its age range in
**years** in the eligibility text while its structured fields give the same numbers in **days**,
with `stdAges: ['CHILD']`; the pipeline maps the structured fields faithfully, so the published
window is a neonatal one:

| trial | registry fields | text | published | layer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NCT06342336 | 18 Days - 75 Days | "Age: 18 years to 75 years at the time of consent" | 0.05 - 0.21 y | mapped |
| NCT07106892 | 18 Days - 75 Days | "Aged >= 18 years AND <= 75 years at the time of signing the ICF" | 0.05 - 0.21 y | mapped |
| NCT06776952 | 18 Days - 70 Days | "Aged 18-70 years (inclusive)" | 0.05 - 0.19 y | needs review |

The CHILD tag is why these adult trials are in a paediatric corpus at all; two of the three are
published. A plausibility check - a prose age range in years against a structured range of days,
or any window narrower than a month - would send them to review instead.

## 4. What this suggests for 3.3 and the queue

- The two corpus-wide defects (A, B) are deterministic and worth fixing before the first
  release tag; neither needs a re-map of the corpus, and A changes no CTML.
- The dominant sampled error class is diagnosis scope, and every instance traces to the seed
  (conditions field) or to a diagnosis named in passing. 2.7 quote grounding addresses the
  second; the first needs the conditions seed to be treated as evidence rather than truth when
  the string is not a diagnosis ("Oncology", "GCT", "Localized Cancer").
- The estimated 32% error rate is for **unreviewed** rows, which the index already marks
  `reviewed = 0`. It does not block a release built from `ctml/reviewed` only, which is what
  `utils/release_index.py` does - but it does mean the intended-use statement (6.1) should say
  plainly that an unreviewed hit is a lead for a curator.
- The 4 queued trials with no recognised flag should be triaged, since a curator cannot act
  on them: 2023-504999-25-00, 2023-508129-28-00, NCT05805605, NCT07306299.

## 5. Both defects fixed, 2026-09-29

Same day, at the source and in the existing corpus (details in
[../changelog.md](../changelog.md)):

- **A**: `map_age_numerical` emits `<` for the registry maximum, and
  `review_helper fix-age-operator --apply` rewrote 465 bounds in 465 trials.
  `age_max_inclusive = 1` fell from 496 index rows to 31 - 28 of them in
  `ctml/reviewed`, which the pass leaves alone because each accepted file's
  SHA-256 is logged. Those 28 are what the first release builds from and need
  a curator's decision before 3.3; the other 3 carry curator comments.
- **B**: `text_rules.age_units_implausible` + the `age_units_implausible`
  flag, which routes to review; `review_helper flag-age-units --apply` moved
  the affected trials out of the published layer. It found a fourth the
  sample had missed: **NCT06375161**, registry `18 Weeks - 70 Weeks` against
  "Age between 18-70 years", published as 0.34-1.36 years.

The index was rebuilt after both: 1,146 trials, 913 mapped, 177 needs review,
56 reviewed; the diagnosis and genomic tables are byte-identical. The offline
suite is 645 tests, 25 of them new (`tests/test_age_units.py`).

The remaining findings in section 3 are unfixed: they are mapping judgements
for a curator or, for the diagnosis-scope classes, work that belongs to 2.7
and to the conditions seed.
