# Audit rules (same as the 2026-10-02 audits of nct2ctml)

You audit how a paediatric-oncology trial-matching pipeline encoded clinical trials for
MatchMiner. For each trial you get a dossier: the registry title, conditions, age fields
and full eligibility text, then the PUBLISHED CTML match tree. Judge whether a curator
would change any published criterion.

## How matching works (conventions - NOT errors)
- A patient matches a trial if they satisfy the step `match` tree AND (if arms carry their own
  `match`) at least one arm's tree. `and`/`or` nodes mean what they say.
- `oncotree_primary_diagnosis` matches that OncoTree node AND ALL ITS DESCENDANTS (subtree expansion).
  `_SOLID_` = every solid tumour, `_LIQUID_` = every haematological malignancy.
  A value starting with `!` excludes that diagnosis (and its subtree).
- `age_numerical`: ">=N", "<N" in years. The registry maximumAge "N Years" is published as "<N+1"
  (completed years: "17 Years" = until the 18th birthday). An inclusive/exclusive difference of one
  year at the maximum is NOT an error.
- `genomic`: hugo_symbol + variant_category (Mutation, Copy Number Variation, Structural Variation,
  Any Variation; a leading `!` excludes). `Any Variation` for a stated mutation class is the convention,
  not an error. fusion_partner/protein_change narrow it.
- `genes_not_required` is information only: those genes are NOT matched on. A gene that is
  correctly a risk-group/cohort/example gene belongs there.
- NOT encoded by design, never count as errors: disease status (newly diagnosed / relapsed /
  refractory / remission), performance status, organ function, prior therapy, measurable disease,
  consent, contraception, CNS status, stage, MRD, immunophenotype markers (CD19, GD2, B7-H3 ...
  are antigens, not genes), germline predisposition syndromes when not encodable.
- Populations OncoTree has no code for are not errors (say "borderline" if it matters).
- The pipeline deliberately adds Ganglioneuroblastoma wherever neuroblastoma is named: not an error.

## Verdicts
- `correct`: nothing a curator would change.
- `error`: at least one published criterion a curator would change. Give the EFFECT:
  - `loses`  - some patients the text makes eligible cannot match (missing diagnosis, gene required
    of patients who need not carry it, wrong/over-broad exclusion, age bound narrower than the text,
    criterion that matches nobody).
  - `over-match` - patients the text excludes can match (exclusion not written where it has its own
    OncoTree node or gene, too-broad parent that pulls in unrelated entities, age wider than text,
    extra diagnosis the text does not name).
  - `both`.
- `borderline`: a judgement call, an OncoTree limitation, or an error so small a curator might leave it.
Be strict about `loses` (it is the headline number): only when a real, not hypothetical, group of
patients the text admits is shut out. A trivially rare case (e.g. one-in-a-million subtype) is borderline.
An over-match only counts when the text clearly excludes those patients or never includes them.

## Causes (pick the main one; add others in the note)
missing-diagnosis, extra-diagnosis, broad-parent, exclusion-not-written, wrong-exclusion,
age, gene-scope (gene required of all but text gives it for one cohort/route), gene-missing
(an alternative gene/alteration not published), gene-encoding (wrong category/partner/protein change),
gene-extra (gene required that the text does not require), subgroup-exclusion (an exclusion that
applies to one cohort made global), matches-nobody, lineage, other.

## Output
Tab-separated, one line per trial, header:
trial_id	verdict	effect	cause	note
`effect` empty for correct/borderline. The note (one or two sentences) must QUOTE the text that
decides it and name the published criterion at fault, e.g.
`"relapsed ALL or LBL" published as B-ALL only; T-ALL and T-LBL missing.`
For `correct`, a short note of what the trial requires is enough.

Helper: `python <scratch>/onco.py "OncoTree Name"` shows ancestors, children and descendant count;
`python <scratch>/onco.py --find word` lists OncoTree names containing a word.
